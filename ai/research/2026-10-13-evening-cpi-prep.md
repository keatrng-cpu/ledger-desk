  - Core: +0.2% m/m (prior 0.3%), 2.5% y/y (prior 2.4%)
  
  Source: https://app.bigdata.com/documents/4AEB3C92CAB42D1E8453D73EDE8BD5C3?cnum=1
  
  Other sources: FXStreet shows 3.7% headline in one note and 3.6% in another (https://app.bigdata.com/documents/F9E78E3691E455CE16F0A4B8F23604A9?cnum=2 , https://app.bigdata.com/documents/2DD72E10205455E33240860AB71BA0B9?cnum=1). Tekedia and IDX Channel show ~3.7%. CNBC (Oct 12) cites FactSet core at 2.4% y/y. **Headline consensus therefore varies 3.6–3.7% depending on the source.**
- **Why headline (3.6%) and core (2.5%) differ.** Headline includes energy. Gasoline and energy are cited as the driver of the headline jump (Scotiabank via MT Newswires; Tekedia; IDX Channel). Core excludes food and energy. Brent was ~$104.6 and WTI ~$92.2 on Oct 13 premarket (Eastmoney) https://app.bigdata.com/documents/798CA7551343CB31087261D09F4C298E?cnum=1. Both numbers are internally consistent: an energy shock with core contained.
- **Macro backdrop**
  - The Fed raised rates to 3.75–4.00% last month. FOMC meets Oct 27–28. Collins sees another hike before the end of 2026. (AOL) https://app.bigdata.com/documents/0A9EF6A2445D1891326B1480AD4F07A4?cnum=2
  - Markets price ~76% odds of a hold this month. (AOL) https://app.bigdata.com/documents/3C68BD95A5FA620BE26FFFAA432240EA?cnum=2
  - DXY is near an 18-month high and the 10-year yield is ~5.2%. (FXStreet, as above)
- **History: how stocks react to CPI surprises**
  - Applied Economics Letters (Taylor & Francis, 2026) studied 48 CPI releases from 2021–2025. Cooler-than-expected prints produced a significant AR[0] of +0.88% and a CAR[0,1] of +1.19%. Hotter prints were negative but not statistically significant. https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13504851.2026.2624038
  - Schwert (1981, Journal of Finance) found a small negative stock reaction to unexpected inflation.
  - SSRN 4280699 finds a robustly negative reaction, largest when inflation expectations are high.
  - Takeaway: the reaction is asymmetric in the recent sample. These are sample statistics, not forecasts.
